Prohibited  Use of AI is prohibited on this assignment.  
 
In this Module, you will learn about a wide range of deep learning applications. Part of 
the learning will be online, during in-class lectures and when attempting assessments, 
but you will really experience hands-on work in your final project. For it you need to 
choose wisely a project that fits your interests. One that would be both motivating and 
technically challenging.  Your project should be an application project. Pick an 

 
 
 
application that interests you and explore how best to apply learning algorithms to solve 
it. 
This project is summative and assesses your ability to design, implement, and evaluate 
a deep-learning pipeline within the syllabus scope. You will be marked on technical 
correctness, experimental design, analysis/communication, and project 
management/reproducibility. 
You shall create an end-to-end deep learning pipeline using a publicly available dataset. 
Your dataset choice should be aligned with an application problem. 
Implementation Constraints 
• Platform: Google Colab (free tier only) 
• Framework: PyTorch or TensorFlow/Keras 
• Dataset: Must be publicly available and accessible via standard libraries or direct 
download 
• Computational Limits: Work within Colab's session limits (~12 hours, limited GPU 
access) 
• Code: All code must be well-documented, reproducible, and include clear 
markdown explanations. All code should run without assuming installation of any 
package by evaluator. In case any package is needed to be installed, use in-
notebook pip commands. For example, if Google colab does not have nltk 
package, your notebook should have !pip install nltk command running in a cell. 
Architecture Requirements 
You must implement and compare at least two different approaches from the following 
categories. Some example comparisons are given in mentioned three categories. You 
can also choose any comparison of your choice. 
Category 1: Classical vs Deep Learning 
• Classical ML method (Logistic Regression, SVM) vs Neural Network (MLP) 
Category 2: Different Neural Architectures 
• Feedforward Networks (MLP) vs Recurrent Networks (RNN/LSTM/GRU) 
• Autoencoders vs Variational Autoencoders 
• RNN vs Transformer-based models 
Category 3: Architecture Variations 
 
 
 
• Different RNN types (vanilla RNN vs LSTM vs GRU) 
• Various autoencoder configurations 
• Different attention mechanisms or transformer variants 
 
Project Examples: 
Choose any dataset of your choice which is publicaly available and apply your choice of 
architectural comparison on that dataset. Some example datasets from different 
domains and potential comparisons are mentioned. You can also apply any different 
comparison for given datasets.  
1. Natural Language Processing 
A. Sentiment Analysis & Text Classification 
• Dataset: IMDb Movie Reviews, Amazon Product Reviews, or Twitter Sentiment 
• Approaches: Compare Logistic Regression (TF-IDF) vs LSTM vs Transformer 
B. Text Generation & Language Modeling 
• Dataset: Gutenberg texts, song lyrics, or poetry datasets 
• Approaches: Character-level RNN vs word-level LSTM vs GPT-style 
transformer 
C. Text Summarization 
• Dataset: CNN/DailyMail, BBC News Summary, or scientific paper abstracts 
• Approaches: Extractive (attention-based) vs abstractive (seq2seq) methods 
2. Time Series Analysis & Forecasting 
A. Stock Price/Financial Prediction 
• Dataset: Yahoo Finance, Quandl financial data, or cryptocurrency prices 
• Approaches: Linear regression vs LSTM vs GRU with attention 
B. Weather/Climate Forecasting 
• Dataset: NOAA weather data, climate datasets from Kaggle 
• Approaches: Traditional time series (ARIMA) vs RNN vs CNN-LSTM hybrid 
C. Energy Consumption Prediction 
 
 
 
• Dataset: Household energy consumption, smart meter data 
• Approaches: Classical regression vs sequence models vs autoencoder-decoder 
3. Audio & Signal Processing 
A. Music Genre Classification 
• Dataset: GTZAN, Free Music Archive (FMA), or Million Song Dataset 
• Approaches: Feature-based SVM vs CNN (on spectrograms) vs RNN (on 
sequences) 
B. Speech/Audio Analysis 
• Dataset: LibriSpeech, Common Voice, or environmental sound datasets 
• Approaches: Classical ML on audio features vs deep networks on 
spectrograms/waveforms 
4. Recommendation Systems 
A. Movie/Content Recommendation 
• Dataset: MovieLens, Netflix Prize data, or book ratings 
• Approaches: Matrix factorization vs autoencoder vs deep collaborative filtering 
B. Music/Playlist Recommendation 
• Dataset: Last.fm, Spotify datasets, or Million Song Dataset 
• Approaches: Content-based vs collaborative filtering vs hybrid deep models 
5. Tabular Data & Structured Learning 
A. Healthcare/Medical Prediction 
• Dataset: Heart disease, diabetes, or cancer datasets from UCI/Kaggle 
• Approaches: Logistic regression vs deep neural networks vs ensemble methods 
B. Economic/Business Analytics 
• Dataset: Customer churn, sales forecasting, or marketing campaign data 
• Approaches: Traditional ML vs deep networks vs generative models for data 
augmentation

Submitting Assessments 
For this assignment: 
• Prepare a Jupyter Notebook file that contains the code and prepare a PDF file with 
the text and data visualisations, technical report. 
• The notebook should be developed using Python 3 within a Jupyter environment, 
with well-commented code and appropriately structured text cells resembling a blog 
format.

Extenuating Circumstances  
The University’s Extenuating Circumstances (ECs) procedure is in place if there are 
genuine circumstances that may prevent a student from submitting an assessment. If 
the EC application is successful, there will be no academic penalty for missing the 
published submission deadline.  
Students are normally expected to apply for ECs in advance of the assessment 
deadline. Students may apply for consideration of ECs retrospectively if they can 
provide evidence that they could not have done so in advance of the deadline. All 
applications for ECs must be supported by independent evidence. 
Successful EC applications for live oral assessments, including vivas, will result in a 
deferral of the oral to be organized by faculty, students, and Timetabling for a date as 
close as possible to the original presentation date. The deadline for supplementary 
materials, if assigned, will be carried forward by the length of the oral assessment 
extension.  
Missing an oral assessment, including a compulsory viva, without an approved EC will 
result in a non-submission for the entire assessment and, accordingly, a recorded mark 
of 0%. 
 
 
 
Students are reminded that the ECs procedure covers only short-term issues (within 21 
days leading to the submission deadline) and that if they experience longer-term 
matters that impact on learning then they must contact Student Support for advice.  
Under the Extenuating Circumstances Policy, students may defer an assessed element 
on only one occasion and may request an extension on a maximum of two occasions.  
For further information, please refer to the Extenuating Circumstances Policy in the 
Academic Handbook.  
 
Academic Misconduct  
You must submit work for assessment purposes that is your own and meets good 
academic practice. Assessments must be completed strictly in accordance with the 
instructions outlined in the assessment brief. This includes ensuring that your work is 
appropriately referenced.  
It is important to understand if artificial intelligence (AI) is permitted to be used or not, 
and if yes, the conditions for its use. For clarity, the assessment brief expressly states if 
AI can be used, and the parameters of its permitted usage. You are not permitted to use 
AI (in any form) if the assessment brief does not expressly state that usage is permitted. 
Any concerns with the academic integrity of a submission will be addressed in 
accordance with the Academic Misconduct Policy. This policy gives details of the 
different types of misconduct and the steps the University may take when a concern 
arises. Action may result in academic penalties being applied directly to you. You are 
advised to review this policy in full before completing and submitting any work for 
marking purposes. The University may also take steps to ascertain the authenticity of a 
submitted piece of work in cases of relevant concern, such as by holding a viva.
